# CFO Crash Course — Bonus Module: The SEC Filing Universe
### Every filing you'll encounter, ranked hugest → lowest probability
Supplement to the DTIL 10-K deep-dive. Examples drawn from the gene-editing peer set:
**DTIL** (Precision BioSciences) · **PRME** (Prime Medicine) · **BEAM** (Beam Therapeutics) · **EDIT** (Editas) · **CRSP** (CRISPR Therapeutics) · **NTLA** (Intellia).

> The one fact that reorganizes everything below: a cash-burning clinical biotech is a **serial issuer of equity**. So after the "big four" periodic reports, the filings you'll touch most are the **capital-raising** ones (S-3 shelf → 424B5 takedowns → ATM). A profitable industrial company would rank these far lower. For you, they're daily bread.

## TIER 1 — You'll touch these constantly

**8-K — Current Report.** The "breaking news" filing; due within **4 business days** of a material event. In biotech this is your workhorse: earnings releases (Item 2.02), material agreements/collaborations (1.01), equity or debt financings (1.01/3.02), executive changes (5.02), and Reg FD disclosures of clinical data (7.01/8.01).
*Example:* **NTLA** drops 8-Ks around clinical readouts (e.g., its in-vivo CRISPR programs); **DTIL** files them for collaboration deals (e.g., its Lilly HBV / ELIMINATE-B work) and each financing. *Contrast:* **CRSP** does **not** file 8-Ks — as a foreign private issuer it uses **6-K** (see Tier 5).

**10-Q — Quarterly Report.** Unaudited financials + MD&A for Q1–Q3 (the Q4 story lives in the 10-K). Due 40 days after quarter-end (accelerated filers). Your recurring cadence: burn rate, cash runway, R&D by program, going-concern language if runway < 12 months.

**10-K — Annual Report.** The full audited annual — the document your DTIL deep-dive is built on. Risk factors, audited statements, MD&A, controls (SOX 302/404), critical audit matters. Due 60–90 days after fiscal year-end depending on filer status.

**Form 4 — Insider Transaction.** *This one is personal to you.* Section 16 officers and directors report each transaction (grants, vests, sales, option exercises) within **2 business days**. As CFO you'll file your own, review the CEO's and directors', and own the compliance calendar (10b5-1 plans, blackout windows).

## TIER 2 — The capital-raising machine (a biotech's lifeblood)

**S-3 — Shelf Registration.** The gas tank. Registers a pool of securities (say $300M) you can sell over ~3 years without a new full registration each time. Requires a ~$75M public float (or the "baby shelf" 1/3-of-float limit below that). Every peer here runs a shelf.

**424B5 — Prospectus Supplement.** The actual **takedown** from the shelf — a follow-on offering or an ATM sale priced off that S-3. This is the filing that hits when the company raises. **BEAM** and **NTLA**-type names file these repeatedly; each one is dilutive and market-moving.

**ATM (At-The-Market) program.** Not its own form — set up via a sales agreement (disclosed on 8-K) and sold under the S-3/424B5. Lets you dribble stock into the market at prevailing prices. The quiet, continuous dilution lever most clinical biotechs keep armed.

**S-1 — Registration Statement.** The IPO document (once), and later used for **resale** registrations (e.g., registering PIPE shares for investors to resell). *Example:* **PRME** registered its IPO on an S-1.

**Form D — Reg D Exempt Offering.** The notice filed for a **private placement / PIPE** — a fast, unregistered raise to institutions, common in gene editing when the public window is shut. Usually paired with a later S-1/S-3 resale registration.

## TIER 3 — Governance & ownership

**DEF 14A — Definitive Proxy Statement.** Annual-meeting playbook: director elections, say-on-pay, auditor ratification, and — critically for biotech — **equity-plan share increases** (your option pool runs dry fast at these burn rates). Preceded by a **PRE 14A** (preliminary).

**S-8 — Employee Equity Registration.** Registers shares issued under stock plans. Biotech runs on stock-based comp, so S-8s recur as pools are topped up. Ties directly to the ASC 718 SBC line you learned in the 10-K.

**SC 13D / 13G — Beneficial Ownership (filed *about* your stock).** Anyone crossing **5%** files: **13G** for passive/institutional holders (index funds), **13D** for active/activist intent. You don't file these — you **monitor** them for your cap table and IR.

**Form 3 / Form 5.** Form 3 = an insider's **initial** ownership statement (filed when you become CFO). Form 5 = the **annual true-up** for anything missed. Bookends to the Form 4 you'll file all year.

**Form 144.** Notice of **proposed sale** of restricted/control securities — filed by insiders before selling. Runs alongside the Form 4 that reports the completed sale.

**13F.** Institutional managers (>$100M AUM) report holdings quarterly. You read these (not file them) to track who owns you.

## TIER 4 — Event-driven & special situations

**PRE 14A / DEF 14A (special meeting).** A special stockholder vote for structural actions: **authorized-share increases** (to keep the dilution machine legal), **reverse stock splits**, or approving an M&A deal.
*Example:* **DTIL** ran a **reverse stock split** to cure a Nasdaq minimum-bid deficiency — a classic small-cap-biotech special-proxy event, followed by 8-K mechanics.

**S-4 — Business Combination.** Registers shares issued in a stock M&A deal; doubles as the merger proxy/prospectus. Rare, but sector M&A is live (your own pulse flagged AbbVie/Apogee, Ipsen/Kartos).

**Schedule TO + SC 14D-9.** Tender-offer documents (bidder's TO; target's 14D-9 response) — the cash-deal cousin of the S-4.

**SC 13E-3.** Going-private transaction disclosure — heightened fairness requirements when insiders/affiliates take the company private.

**NT 10-K / NT 10-Q (Form 12b-25).** "We'll be late" notice — buys 15 days (10-K) / 5 days (10-Q). A yellow flag to the market; you want to never need it.

**Form 8-A.** Registers your class of securities under the Exchange Act at listing — the plumbing that makes you a reporting company. Filed once, near the IPO.

**Form 25 / Form 15.** Terminal filings: **25** delists from the exchange; **15** deregisters/suspends reporting. You hope to never sign these.

---

## TIER 5 — Foreign & edge cases

**20-F / 6-K — Foreign Private Issuer reports.** The big fork. If the company qualifies as an FPI, it files a **20-F** (annual, the 10-K analog) and **6-K** (interim/current, the 10-Q + 8-K analog) — and is exempt from Section 16 (no Form 3/4/5) and proxy rules.
*Canonical peer example:* **CRSP (CRISPR Therapeutics) is Swiss-domiciled** → it files **20-F and 6-K**, not 10-K/10-Q/8-K. If you ever benchmark CRSP's disclosures against DTIL's, you're comparing two different regimes. Know which lens you're in.

**Reg A / Form 1-A and friends.** Small-offering and niche cross-border forms — you'll almost never touch these as a Nasdaq-listed biotech CFO.

## THE BIOTECH FINANCING LIFECYCLE (how the filings chain together)

```
  IPO                 STAY CURRENT              RAISE REPEATEDLY            ENDGAME
  ───                 ────────────              ───────────────            ───────
  S-1  ─►  8-A  ─►    10-K / 10-Q / 8-K   ─►    S-3 (shelf)                S-4  (get acquired)
  (register) (list)   DEF 14A (govern)          │                          or
                      Form 3/4/5 (insiders)     ├─► 424B5 (follow-on)      25 / 15 (delist)
                                                ├─► ATM (drip)
                                                └─► Form D (PIPE) ─► S-1 resale
```

## HOW TO READ ANY FILING FAST (CFO triage)
1. **Form type = the question it answers.** 8-K "what just happened?"; 10-Q/K "how are we doing?"; S-3/424B5 "we're raising"; 13D/G "who's buying us?"; DEF 14A "what do we need voted?".
2. **Cover page + first item** carries 80% of the signal (the 8-K Item number tells you the whole story).
3. **Follow the cash and the share count** — every biotech filing ultimately moves runway or dilution.
4. **Check the filer regime first** — 10-K world (DTIL/BEAM/NTLA/EDIT/PRME) vs 20-F world (CRSP). It changes what even exists.
